# Infographic / Explainer

Explain a product's mechanism, specifications, or process through simple diagrams and evidence. One understandable idea per scene.

## Visual system

- Start with paper `#FAF9F6`, ink `#172033`, blue `#2563EB`, and teal `#0F766E`; map colors consistently to concepts and adapt to the brand.
- Use a readable sans, consistent icon stroke weight, short labels, and clearly separated diagram groups.
- Choose simple arrows, numbered steps, annotated product crops, or genuinely supported charts. The product should anchor the explanation rather than disappear behind graphics.
- Keep chart labels and units visible. Do not rely on color alone to distinguish categories.

## Motion grammar at 30 fps

- Diagram buildup: 10–18 frames per meaningful element, with 4–8 frame stagger when introducing related parts.
- Hold a completed relationship for 1.5–3 seconds depending on label length.
- Animate a numeric value only when the value and unit are verified. Keep axis limits and proportions honest throughout the animation.
- Trace flow along a path or highlight a component as it is explained. Avoid spinning icons without a narrative purpose.
- Transition by retaining a shared component or idea between scenes, rather than replacing the whole screen after every sentence.

## Product story: adaptable 30-second example

| Time | Purpose | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3s | Question | State the product problem or mechanism to explain |
| 3–8s | Product context | Annotated hero view and the main concept |
| 8–22s | Explanation | Two or three labeled steps with synchronized voice |
| 22–27s | Benefit | Connect the mechanism to a supported outcome |
| 27–30s | CTA | One clear action and product identifier |

## Inputs and female voice

Use product photos for callouts, genuine specs for comparisons, and real UI for software explanations. If no data exists, use a conceptual diagram instead of inventing a statistic or a competitor comparison. Label a simplified schematic as illustrative when it could be mistaken for a literal cutaway.

Voice: clear, conversational female delivery, roughly 125–145 words/minute. Pause around numbers, units, and process steps. Use plain language before technical terminology.

## Preview and quality checks

Show one complete cause-and-effect or step diagram, with an associated product detail and female narration. Check label legibility, relationship accuracy, actual units, and whether the idea remains understandable without sound.

## Reference improvements

### Optional variation: data/filter reveal

Use a field of illustrative items, reveal a real filter condition, and isolate the relevant result. Keep sample dots distinct from measured quantities and keep units/sources beside real statistics. For vertical video, show one result or metric at a time. See the [VAINU visual study](../saas-product-demo/references/2026-11-14-data-intelligence.md); exact filtering motion was not verified.

Save observed diagram choreography, visual analogies, and annotation techniques. Source data must still come from the user's actual product; a reference's attractive chart is not evidence for new product claims. See [reference learning](../../references/reference-learning.md).

### Optional variation: focused metric card

Use a restrained card, one clear annotation, and a single emphasized point/bar to communicate a real metric. For AmezPrice, use genuine history/score data with correct price units and context. Weather probabilities and other apps' figures do not become price-confidence claims. The [weather-card study](references/2026-11-14-focused-metric-card.md) supports a visual hierarchy idea; its exact chart animation and claimed frame rate are unverified.
