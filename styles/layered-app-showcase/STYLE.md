## Visual system: proposed production recipe

These settings are starting choices for new work, not sampled colors or measured parameters from the source.

- Build natively at 1080 × 1920, 9:16. Use the actual app palette. If the user requests a reference-inspired mint variant, a possible starting palette is deep teal `#126D67`, mint `#C9F3E7`, and an off-white card surface `#F7FBFA`.
- Give one primary UI card the clearest contrast and strongest focus. Use two or three supporting cards to suggest feature breadth, then let them recede before detailed reading.
- Separate planes through overlap, soft shadows, size, and controlled perspective. A subtle translucent support shape or brand-colored ribbon is optional; it should not cover text or imply an actual app control.
- Keep UI labels and real content intact. Do not crop off a control needed to understand the scene. Avoid packing a full multi-column dashboard onto a phone-sized card.
- Place a short approved benefit above or below the UI, reserving the shared caption and CTA safe areas. Make the app identifier visible early and at the ending.

## Motion direction: proposed and preview-tested later

Use a code-based 2.5D plane treatment when real screenshots are available; a true 3D scene is optional when suitable assets and tooling exist. Do not claim the source used a particular implementation.

- Introduce the card group, then settle on a single feature card. At 30 fps, a proposed initial group reveal is 18–30 frames, with small supporting-card offsets rather than every card entering at once.
- Move toward a readable frontal pose before presenting small text. Suggested reading tilt is no more than roughly 8–12 degrees; reduce it further for dense UI.
- Let the next relevant card advance while the previous one becomes support. Keep one consistent depth order and transition vocabulary. Avoid cards passing through one another or clipping at the frame edges.
- Hold important content according to its reading length and recorded narration. Decorative drift should not make a button, balance, label, or feature unreadable.
- Demonstrate taps or value changes only when the real product evidence supports them. A floating screenshot alone is a visual showcase, not a recorded interactive demo.

These are original animation proposals. The reference's exact easing, entrance order, travel paths, and soundtrack sync remain unverified.

## Adaptable 30-second app story

| Time | Purpose | Suggested treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3s | Hook | Approved app benefit with a clear app identifier |
| 3–8s | Hero | Actual home/feature view comes into focus |
| 8–18s | Feature breadth | Two or three supported features, one primary card at a time |
| 18–25s | Payoff | One real workflow result or readable feature outcome |
| 25–30s | CTA | Return to a stable hero with one approved next step |

This is a suggested new production plan. Change the duration when requested; do not stretch a sparse app brief just to fill 30 seconds or copy the source's duration automatically.

## Natural female voice and sound

Use a friendly, assured female voice with one concise benefit per feature scene. Start around 130–150 words/minute and adjust to the actual language and reading load. Leave breathing room when a new card comes forward. Follow the shared human-recording/AI-voice distinction; no source voice was verified.

## Preview and quality checks

After explicit user selection, make the shared 6–8 second 540 × 960 preview with a recognizable app hero, one supporting-card change, one understandable feature, and actual female narration when available. Use the user's own app assets. Do not call a source-reference animation an Amezprice preview.

Check text size, perspective distortion, overlap, shadow consistency, card clipping, caption clearance, and whether the real app feature is clear. Validate the actual rendered output and listen to its narration before final delivery. This guide is not evidence that a preview has already passed those checks.
