# Bold Social Media

Strong first-frame communication, vivid product visuals, and purposeful fast edits for a phone screen. Energy should make the product memorable.

## Visual system

- Use a contrasting brand-led palette; a starting combination is near-black `#101010`, cream `#FFF7ED`, coral `#FF563F`, and yellow `#FFE14A`. Use only the colors needed for hierarchy.
- Use a licensed bold face with short headlines and readable numerals. Give offers and product names a stable, clear treatment.
- Combine product cutouts, blocks, crop changes, and simple badges. Keep visual anchors consistent across quick cuts.
- Put the product in the hook whenever practical. Avoid letting graphic stickers consume the product area.

## Motion grammar at 30 fps

- Main entrances: 5–10 frames with a controlled settle. Keep small labels still while they are read.
- Cut approximately every 0.6–1.5 seconds during energetic sections, then hold longer for offers or important claims. Adjust to actual content; do not treat this as a mandatory cut grid.
- Use 4–8 frame swipes, masks, or shape matches. A few recurring transition rules create more coherence than a different effect on every cut.
- Align accents with real music/voice events. Avoid repeated full-screen flashes and shaky text.
- Finish with at least one clear CTA hold rather than filling the last second with a rapid montage.

## Product story: adaptable 30-second example

| Time | Purpose | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2s | Hook | Big benefit phrase plus a recognizable product |
| 2–7s | Reveal | Product name, vivid crop, first feature |
| 7–21s | Benefits | Fast, purposeful views with concise benefit cards |
| 21–26s | Payoff/offer | Only a verified offer or supported product result |
| 26–30s | CTA | Hold the action, product, and useful destination |

## Inputs and female voice

Images can become alternating product crops and layered cutouts; clips can supply real product action. An offer badge is optional and must have real price/discount details. Never invent urgency, stock counts, or endorsements to fill the format.

Voice: bright, confident female delivery, roughly 145–165 words/minute. Keep the tone conversational and product-specific; avoid shouted, nonstop sales copy. Duck music during narration.

## Preview and quality checks

Show the hook, a visible product, a benefit card, and the transition rhythm. Check readability without pausing, whether the product name is remembered, and whether speech remains clear through sound effects.

## Reference improvements

### Optional variation: type wall and packshot

Use alternating outline/solid words as a background motif, followed by a clear product hero. In vertical layouts, reduce line count and preserve one readable benefit rather than duplicating a crowded poster. Use the user's own copy and brand colors. See the [mango reference](references/2026-11-14-mango-type-packshot.md); typography treatment is clearer in its poster than in its tiny video samples.

### Optional variation: ingredient and packshot

Frame a large product with a few relevant, verified ingredient/material accents and a concise headline. Keep packaging unobstructed and show only ingredients that belong to the actual product. For an app, borrow visual hierarchy only when useful; do not import unrelated fruit or beverage props. See the [lime-drink reference](references/2026-11-14-lime-packshot.md). Exact motion timing is unverified.

Save specific hook layouts, crop rhythms, and transition pairings with timestamps. Make experimental techniques optional until they improve a real product preview. See [reference learning](../../references/reference-learning.md).
