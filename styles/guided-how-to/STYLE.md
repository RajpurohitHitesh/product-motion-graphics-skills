# Guided / How-To Tutorial

**Choice 2.** Teach one app task so the viewer can repeat it: where to start, what to select, what to do next, and how to recognize the result. ## Visual system

- Keep the current screen in a stable, mostly frontal view. Orient the viewer before cropping to a small control.
- Give each action a short numbered instruction, such as a step number plus the actual control/action. A simple progress marker is optional when there are several steps.
- Use one tap/cursor cue and one active highlight at a time. Match the cue to the control's actual position and input type; do not add a desktop cursor to a mobile tap without purpose.
- Keep the highlighted control and the next/result screen visually connected. Retain useful surrounding UI so the viewer knows where the control is.
- Use brand-compatible colors for annotations and readable captions. Avoid floating card fans, large tilt, repeated flashes, or decorative overlays during an instruction.

Use the shared 1080 × 1920, 9:16 layout. Maintain caption space and readable UI when the screenshot aspect ratio differs; crop/reframe deliberately rather than stretching it.

## Instruction and screenshot evidence

Plan each teaching beat as **current screen → verified action → supplied or otherwise supported next state**. For every action, identify the real control and the evidence for the transition: ordered screenshots with a confirmed action, a recording, current documentation, or the user's explanation.

Do not infer a whole task from a single isolated screenshot. With one image, show only a bounded verified step or explain what is visible. If a necessary result/control is missing, request that specific screen or action detail; do not fabricate a saved alert, success toast, navigation route, or completed task.

Screenshot transitions and tap cues are an illustrated walkthrough. Describe that source honestly in the handoff. Label illustrative values or reconstructed states where they could be mistaken for actual product behavior; supplied genuine states do not need repetitive production disclaimers.

## Motion grammar: original starting points at 30 fps

- Orient on the complete screen before a focus move. Suggested crop movement: 10–16 frames with smooth deceleration.
- Highlight the next target briefly before the tap cue, then let the viewer register it. A proposed cue takes 8–12 frames; keep it subordinate to the instruction.
- Transition to the supported next screenshot with a restrained cut or 6–10 frame dissolve. Preserve matching landmarks when possible.
- Hold each meaningful action/result long enough to hear and follow the spoken instruction; 2–4 seconds is a starting range, not a mandatory grid.
- Stop decorative movement during reading, data entry, and result confirmation. Match all cues to the real voice timing.

Avoid saving time by racing through essential steps. Shorten the task scope or use the user's requested longer duration when necessary.

## Adaptable 30-second task story

| Time | Purpose | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3s | Goal | State the one task the viewer will learn |
| 3–6s | Starting point | Show the real starting screen and prerequisites |
| 6–23s | Actions | Usually two to four verified steps, each with its next state |
| 23–27s | Result | Show how the viewer can recognize completion |
| 27–30s | Finish | Brief recap or useful next step |

The actual task determines step count and duration. Change framing, instructional wording, and annotations to fit the screenshots while preserving required action order. Creativity improves teaching; it must not reorder dependencies or change the app.

## Natural female voice

Use patient, conversational female narration, starting around 115–140 words/minute. Give one action per phrase, say the control label accurately, then leave a pause to locate it. Synchronize the tap cue and next screen with the recorded instruction. Preserve the shared real-recording/AI-voice distinction, captions, and missing-audio behavior.

## Preview and checks

After explicit style selection, produce the shared 6–8 second 540 × 960 preview with one complete verified step and its next/result state. Include actual female narration when available. A short step sample is not proof that the full task is documented.

Check that a new viewer can locate the target, read its label, understand the instruction, and connect it to the next screen. Verify cue coordinates after cropping, real step order, screenshot version consistency, result evidence, caption clearance, and narration sync. Keep a missing outcome pending rather than inventing successful completion.
