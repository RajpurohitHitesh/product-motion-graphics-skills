# Minimal / Apple-inspired

Premium product storytelling through space, precise typography, and controlled movement. Borrow restraint and visual clarity, not another brand's logos, slogans, or exact advertising sequence.

## Visual system

- Base: warm white `#F6F5F1`, ink `#151515`, muted gray `#737373`. A dark variant may invert the surface and text. Use the product's brand color for one restrained accent.
- Use a licensed clean sans with a real medium/semibold weight. Keep a single type family, short headlines, and generous spacing. Let the product supply texture and color.
- Give the product approximately half the useful canvas when its shape permits. Center it or use a balanced offset. Preserve a clear silhouette; use soft grounded shadows rather than decorative glow.
- Keep essential messaging to one headline and one supporting line at a time. Do not turn every empty area into a label.

## Motion grammar at 30 fps

- Product entry: 18–30 frames, opacity plus a subtle rise or 0.96 → 1 scale; smooth deceleration without visible bounce.
- Headline entry: 10–18 frames; stagger related lines by 3–5 frames. Hold important copy long enough to read.
- Product drift: about 2–4% over a shot. Match image edges and avoid exposing a cropped background.
- Transitions: 8–14 frame soft reveals or shape-matched cuts. Avoid elastic type, whip pans, and repeated flashes.
- Use fewer, longer product views than the other energetic styles. Motion should lead the eye to a material, detail, or benefit.

## Product story: adaptable 30-second example

| Time | Purpose | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3s | Hook | One short promise over a clean hero image |
| 3–9s | Product reveal | Full product settles into view; brand remains readable |
| 9–21s | Benefits | Two or three real details, with deliberate visual changes |
| 21–26s | Payoff | Return to the strongest product view and supported benefit |
| 26–30s | CTA | Product name, one action, quiet finish |

## Inputs and female voice

For one photo, use alternate crops and a background treatment rather than inventing unseen product geometry. For clips, favor stable views and restrained speed changes. Do not recolor or warp the actual product to fit the palette.

Voice: warm, confident, unhurried female delivery, roughly 120–140 words/minute as a starting point. Leave pauses after the hook and product name. Keep music sparse and below the narration.

## Preview and quality checks

The preview should show the product reveal, a headline entrance, and one quiet transition. Check that the product remains recognizable at phone size, whites retain edge definition, shadows stay attached, and text is legible before the shot changes.
