# Kinetic Typography

Words carry the rhythm while the product and its benefits remain visible. Build timing around recorded speech and genuine musical accents.

## Visual system

- Start with high contrast: charcoal `#141414`, cream `#FFF8ED`, and one accent such as lime `#D7FF3F`. Adapt to the brand rather than forcing the example palette.
- Use a licensed bold or condensed display face with reliable glyphs for the selected language; pair with a simple readable caption face if needed.
- Build hierarchy through scale and weight. Emphasize one important word or phrase per beat. Reserve a consistent product area so large type does not hide packaging or controls.
- Keep intentional kinetic headlines separate from captions. Avoid two competing word-by-word text layers.

## Motion grammar at 30 fps

- Word/phrase entrance: 4–9 frames. A short scale overshoot can settle by 10–14 frames; keep glyphs crisp.
- Use 2–5 word semantic groups for readable phrases. A single emphasized word may pop quickly, but complete claims must remain understandable.
- Use position, scale, mask reveals, and shape-matched words. Limit rotation so Hindi and small letterforms remain readable.
- Anchor entrances to actual voice timing. If music is supplied, inspect its beat locations; do not label arbitrary time intervals as beat-synchronized.
- Allow 1–2 second holds on the central product promise. Constant acceleration makes every word equally weak.

## Product story: adaptable 30-second example

| Time | Purpose | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2s | Hook | A sharp, short question or benefit phrase |
| 2–7s | Product | Product name and hero image land together |
| 7–21s | Benefits | Two or three phrases with coordinated product crops |
| 21–26s | Payoff | Strongest supported benefit gets the biggest hold |
| 26–30s | CTA | One readable action and a clear product view |

## Inputs and female voice

Use a cutout, stable product crop, or clip as the visual anchor. Treat reference typography as a motion example, not as copy to repeat for a different product.

Voice: expressive, articulate female delivery, roughly 145–165 words/minute as an initial range. Do not speed the narration to rescue an overloaded script. For Hindi/Hinglish, preserve meaningful word groups and natural pronunciation.

## Preview and quality checks

Show a recorded spoken phrase with matching text emphasis, a product reveal, and one transition. Check entrances against actual speech, compare sound-on and sound-off comprehension, and verify that captions do not collide with hero words.

## Reference improvements

### Optional graphic treatment: outline/solid type wall

Alternate outline and filled lettering as a low-priority background motif while preserving a single foreground message. Keep density low enough for vertical phone reading. The [mango study](../bold-social-media/references/2026-11-14-mango-type-packshot.md) supports this static design direction; beat sync, word timing, and text motion were not verified.

Add observed phrase transitions, baseline shifts, or emphasis patterns as optional techniques. Record whether timing was measured from audio or only inferred visually. Keep product visibility and readability as constraints. See [reference learning](../../references/reference-learning.md).
